# Blueprint V2

## Product Constitution

### 1. What Blueprint Is

Blueprint is the talent-development operating system for multi-op DJ companies.

It helps an owner:

**HIRE → TRAIN → VERIFY → DEVELOP → ADVANCE**

their people without personally managing every routine step.

Blueprint is not simply an LMS, recruiting CRM, or collection of DJ courses.

It learns how a company operates, helps the owner determine what their people system should look like, builds that system with them, and then runs as much of it automatically as practical.

The long-term outcome is:

**Build the team your DJ company needs without the owner being the system.**



---



# 2. Core Product Philosophy

Blueprint follows several principles throughout the product.

### Blueprint recommends. Humans decide.

Blueprint should use its expertise, company information, and eventually industry benchmarks to make strong recommendations.

The owner can approve or modify those recommendations.

### Blueprint should not require expertise the owner does not have.

Customers should not need to be:

- instructional designers
- HR experts
- compensation experts
- software builders
- training-system architects

When customers don't know how to create something, Blueprint helps them create it.

### Automation handles administration. Humans provide judgment.

Blueprint should automate:

- deadlines
- reminders
- follow-ups
- assignments
- scheduling prompts
- progress tracking
- prerequisite enforcement
- objective grading
- alerts
- documentation

Humans should primarily be involved where human judgment creates value.

### Completion does not equal competency.

Watching training does not prove someone can perform the job.

Blueprint distinguishes:

**Learning → Evidence → Verification → Competency → Advancement**



---



# 3. Company Blueprint

Every customer begins by creating their **Company Blueprint**.

This is not simply account setup.

Blueprint performs a guided diagnosis of the business and uses the results to configure the system.

## Team & Owner Goals

Blueprint learns:

- current number of DJs
- current DJ utilization
- events per DJ
- pricing
- DJ compensation
- reliability and performance issues
- current team pain points
- desired team size
- reason for growth
- owner goals
- desire to stop DJing, scale, expand, improve quality, etc.
- current trainers/mentors
- current organizational capacity

Blueprint should challenge assumptions.

If an owner has five DJs performing 15 events each and wants ten DJs, Blueprint should determine whether recruiting is actually appropriate.

Blueprint should help answer:

**How many DJs does this company actually need?**

## Business & Market

Blueprint learns:

- event volume
- pricing
- packages
- service tiers
- enhancements
- service complexity
- market
- competitors
- market positioning
- growth expectations

Blueprint uses this information to understand the level of talent the company actually needs.

## Economics & Compensation

Blueprint teaches enough business economics for owners to make informed talent decisions.

It should help owners understand:

**Revenue → COGS → Gross Profit → Operating Expenses → Net Profit**

Blueprint helps model:

- DJ compensation
- trainee compensation
- assistant compensation
- training costs
- direct event costs
- equipment expectations
- margins
- workforce economics

Blueprint is not accounting software.

Its purpose is to answer:

**Can this company's economics support the talent model it wants?**

## Workforce Model

Blueprint supports companies using different legitimate workforce structures.

This may include employees and independent contractors.

Blueprint may educate owners about considerations around worker classification but does not make legal classification determinations.

The workforce model changes downstream recruiting, qualification, training, compensation, and development workflows.

# 4. Ideal Candidate & Culture

Blueprint helps companies determine who they should recruit.

This includes deep guided work around:

## Three Core Values

Blueprint helps owners discover three meaningful behavioral core values through interactive exercises and AI-guided questioning.

Values should represent observable behaviors rather than generic words.

Those values become operational data used elsewhere in Blueprint.

## Soft Skills

Owners prioritize traits such as:

- reliability
- coachability
- communication
- confidence
- adaptability
- initiative
- composure
- organization
- empathy
- professionalism
- energy

Blueprint should prevent owners from simply selecting everything.

## Hard Skills

Blueprint distinguishes between:

**Must arrive with**

and

**Can be developed**

Examples include:

- beatmixing
- MC ability
- wedding knowledge
- audio
- equipment setup
- music knowledge
- production skills
- planning/client skills

## Practical Fit

Blueprint considers:

- geographic proximity
- transportation
- weekend availability
- expected event commitment
- schedule flexibility
- equipment requirements

Blueprint synthesizes this into an **Ideal Candidate Profile**.

The owner approves or modifies it.

# 5. Recruiting

Blueprint determines whether the company actually needs additional talent before encouraging recruiting.

When recruiting is appropriate, Blueprint uses the Company Blueprint to create the recruiting system.

This can include:

- recruiting campaign
- job posting
- join/careers-page content
- application
- screening questions
- candidate communications
- deadlines
- reminders
- one-way video interviews
- transcript analysis
- auditions/work samples where appropriate
- human approval points
- hiring presentation frameworks
- meeting/interview guidance

Core values should directly influence behavioral interview questions.

Blueprint analyzes responses against company-defined behavioral expectations but does not make consequential hiring decisions autonomously.

**Humans hire people. Blueprint provides evidence.**

Blueprint may intentionally provide more applicant reminders and recovery opportunities than the operating-company processes that inspired it.

Blueprint customers configure their own strictness level.



---



# 6. One Person, Multiple Journeys

Blueprint maintains one person identity throughout the lifecycle.

A person may progress through:

**Applicant → Candidate → Hired → Active → Alumni**

System permissions are separate from talent roles.

## System Roles

Examples:

- Applicant
- DJ/Trainee
- Trainer/Mentor
- Manager
- Owner/Admin

## Talent Roles

Company-defined examples:

- Assistant DJ
- Wedding Lead DJ
- Trainer
- Mentor
- Bridal Show Representative
- Venue Representative
- Leadership roles

One person may hold multiple talent roles.



---



# 7. Competency-Based Architecture

Courses are not the foundation of Blueprint.

**Competencies are.**

A Talent Role consists of required competencies.

Example:

### Wedding Lead DJ

may require:

- Wedding Flow
- MC
- Music Knowledge
- Beatmixing
- Ceremony Audio
- Client Communication
- Company Standards
- relevant technical competencies

Different companies can require different competency levels.

A competency can be satisfied through different forms of evidence.



---



# 8. Multiple Entry Routes

Blueprint does not force everyone through the same training program.

### Full Training Path

Designed for inexperienced/developable talent.

### Standardization Path

Designed for experienced DJs joining a company.

Existing skills are assessed and demonstrated competencies can be passed off.

Only gaps require development.

### Qualification/Audition Path

May be appropriate for experienced independent talent.

Auditions, mixes, work samples and skills checks may verify existing competencies before company-specific development begins.

The principle is:

**Determine what they already know → identify the gaps → develop only what is missing.**

# 10. Training Logistics

Blueprint manages more than educational content.

It can manage:

### Deadlines

Blueprint creates and manages training schedules, reminders, overdue alerts and escalation.

### Gear

Companies define how trainees access equipment for practice.

Blueprint communicates equipment requirements for relevant assignments.

### Training Pay

Companies configure appropriate compensation structures for training activities.

Blueprint can educate and model options but does not need to become payroll software.

### Human Intervention

Blueprint should tell managers when their attention is required rather than requiring constant dashboard monitoring.

Example:

**Jake has completed MC Foundations and is ready for his live MC test-out.**



---



# 11. Verification & Evidence

Blueprint tracks evidence rather than relying solely on completion.

Evidence can include:

### Video behavior

Blueprint may track meaningful video completion behavior rather than simply whether a lesson was opened.

### Quiz behavior

Blueprint can consider:

- scores
- attempts
- repeated misses
- improvement
- completion behavior

### Assignment performance

Assignments may use:

- self-assessment
- objective grading
- AI-assisted evaluation
- trainer grading
- rubrics

Blueprint should recommend human rubric-based grading when appropriate.

### Real-world evaluation

Trainers/Lead DJs can evaluate trainees using structured rubrics during observations and working events.

### AI evaluation

Multimodal AI may assist with video/audio assignments where useful.

AI evaluation should provide evidence and recommendations rather than pretending subjective DJ performance can always be autonomously judged.



---



# 12. Advancement

Verification and advancement are separate.

Blueprint determines whether required evidence and competencies have been satisfied.

For consequential talent-role advancement, Blueprint presents the evidence to an authorized human.

Example:

**Jake satisfies all Assistant Ready requirements.**

**Approve Advancement**

After approval, Blueprint automatically unlocks the appropriate next development stage.



---



# 13. Real-World Experience

Blueprint is calendar-aware but is not an event-management platform.

Possible event sources include:

- Google Calendar
- calendar/iCal subscription
- manual event entry
- future integrations with scheduling/event platforms

Managers identify events that are appropriate for:

- observation
- assistant training
- supervised lead work
- other development experiences

Blueprint handles the administrative workflow around those experiences.

# 14. Company-Specific Training Creation

Customers often know what their people need to learn but do not know how to create effective training.

Blueprint solves that.

The customer should not face an empty:

**Create Course**

screen.

Instead, Blueprint acts as a **Course Builder Coach**.

## Example: Photo Booth

Blueprint already understands a reasonable instructional structure:

**Overview → Equipment → Setup → Operation → Guest Experience → Troubleshooting → Breakdown → Verification**

The company supplies its equipment-specific knowledge.

Blueprint tells the owner what to film and provides a shot list.

The owner records their process.

AI can then help produce:

- transcription
- lesson text
- procedures
- quizzes
- checklists
- troubleshooting content
- assignments
- practical test-outs

Blueprint recommends where the new training belongs in the company's development path.

**Blueprint provides the training architecture. The company provides the company-specific knowledge. AI bridges the two.**

Blueprint supports ongoing talent development.

Possible tools include:

- recurring development check-ins
- post-event reflections
- SWOT reviews

- performance-review frameworks
- skill goals
- continuing education
- new-service training
- equipment training
- competency re-verification
- video upload and review

Its boundary is:

**Blueprint owns talent development, not everyday employment administration.**

# 16. Advancement Beyond DJ

Companies may develop people toward:

- Trainer
- Mentor
- Bridal Show Representative
- Venue Representative
- advanced technical roles
- leadership roles
- company-defined roles

Blueprint provides the engine for defining and developing these roles.

It does not provide protected operating-company recipes.

# 17. Process Builder

Customers have different levels of maturity.

Whenever Blueprint needs a company process, the customer should generally have three options:

### Recommend One For Me

Blueprint creates a recommended starting framework.

### Show Me How Others Do It

Blueprint may provide Blueprint-created frameworks, anonymized patterns, benchmarks or explicitly shared community templates.

### I Already Have A Process

Blueprint helps capture, structure, improve and automate the customer's existing process.

Blueprint should never assume the customer already knows how to design a good process.



---



# 18. Blueprint Master Library

Blueprint maintains global content including:

- DJ curriculum
- courses
- lessons
- competencies
- role templates
- rubrics
- recruiting frameworks
- development frameworks
- review templates
- process templates

Customers may use Blueprint content as-is.

If customers customize master content, Blueprint creates a company-specific version rather than modifying the master.

Blueprint master content is versioned.

Updates must never silently change historical evidence or completed training.

# 19. Company Data

Each customer's operational world is private.

Company-owned data includes:

- Company Blueprint
- economics
- pay structures
- people
- applicants
- interviews
- transcripts
- company content
- evaluations
- development paths
- evidence
- advancement
- reviews
- company-specific processes

Tenant isolation must be enforced at the database authorization layer.

Company A must never be able to access Company B data.



---



# 20. Community Knowledge

Blueprint may eventually support customer-contributed templates and processes.

Sharing must be explicit and opt-in.

Company processes are never automatically converted into community content.

Community contributions should be sanitized of company-specific/private information before publication.



---



# 21. Operating-Company IP Firewall

Blueprint is separate from the founder's operating DJ companies.

Protected internal processes, data, prompts, methodologies and competitive advantages do not automatically enter Blueprint.

Examples may include proprietary:

- recruiting methodology
- advertising strategy
- market-expansion systems
- certification methodology
- weekly development/coaching processes
- show-review processes
- venue relationship strategies
- bridal-show processes
- internal scoring systems

Blueprint may extract universal principles and independently create general frameworks.

**Blueprint contains the engine. The operating company keeps its recipes.**

A useful product test is:

**Would we be comfortable selling this exact Blueprint functionality to a direct competitor in one of our own markets?**

If not, the functionality must be generalized or excluded.



---



# 22. Multi-Tenant Architecture

Blueprint is one multi-tenant SaaS platform.

Global Blueprint content exists separately from tenant-owned operational data.

Tenant operational records are scoped to a company.

Authorization is enforced at the database layer.

AI context must follow the same authorization boundaries.

When AI acts for Company A, it may receive:

**Blueprint global knowledge + authorized Company A context**

It may never receive:

**Company B private data or protected operating-company data.**



---



# 23. Golden Paths

The application must support at minimum:

### New DJ

Recruit → Hire → Full Development → Real-World Experience → Verification → Human Advancement.

### Experienced DJ

Recruit/Import → Assessment → Existing Competencies Verified → Gap Development → Advancement.

### Existing Team Member

Add → Standardization Assessment → Development → Ongoing Development.

### Company Without A Training System

Diagnose → Recommend Roles/Competencies/Paths → Owner Approves → Blueprint Builds System.

### Company With Existing Training

Capture → Structure → Identify Gaps → Owner Approves → Blueprint Runs System.

### New Company-Specific Skill

Describe Need → Blueprint Provides Training Architecture → Owner Records Knowledge → AI Builds Supporting Content → Owner Approves → Blueprint Adds To Development System.

### Post-Launch Development

Lead DJ → Check-In/Review → Development Need → Goal/Training → Evidence → Improvement.

### Tenant Isolation

Company A and Company B use the same product while their private data and AI context remain inaccessible to each other.



---



# 24. Product Invariants

These rules may not be casually changed by implementation agents.

1. **Course completion does not equal competency.**
2. **Competency requires defined evidence.**
3. **Experienced people can demonstrate existing competency without unnecessary retraining.**
4. **Consequential talent-role advancement requires authorized human approval.**
5. **Blueprint recommends; the company decides.**
6. **Companies may use different paths toward equivalent roles.**
7. **Company customization never changes Blueprint master content.**
8. **Master-content updates never silently rewrite historical training/evidence.**
9. **AI cannot access another tenant's private context.**
10. **Tenant isolation must exist below the UI layer.**
11. **Protected operating-company IP never automatically enters Blueprint.**
12. **Customers should not need instructional-design expertise to create effective training.**
13. **Routine administration should be automated whenever practical.**
14. **Humans should be pulled into the system primarily when judgment matters.**
15. **System permissions and company talent roles are separate concepts.**
16. **One person's identity should persist across recruiting, training and development rather than being recreated at every stage.**



---

Blueprint is not intended to become:

- a full CRM
- event-management software
- payroll
- accounting software
- general HRIS
- scheduling software
- a generic LMS for every industry

Blueprint's domain is:

# Building DJ Talent

It helps DJ companies determine:

**Who do we need?**

**Who should we recruit?**

**How should we hire them?**

**What do they need to learn?**

**What can they already do?**

**How should they develop?**

**How do we know they're actually capable?**

**When are they ready?**

**What should they become next?**

That is Blueprint V2.
